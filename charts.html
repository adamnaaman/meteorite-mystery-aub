01 Where are the mapped records?

Of 32,186 meteorite entries with usable locations, 22,099 were recorded at or south of 60°S. The remaining 10,087 mapped records are elsewhere. Another 13,530 entries cannot be mapped reliably.

The 68.7% share uses only the 32,186 mappable records, not all catalog entries and not actual meteorite fall events.

02 Found later or witnessed falling?

Found means recovered after falling; Fell means the fall was witnessed. The catalog contains far more finds than observed falls.

Every mapped Antarctic-latitude record in this historical snapshot is classified as Found. That is a catalog property, not evidence that no meteorites ever fell there while someone was watching.

03 Explore the recovery map

Zoom, pan, and hover over geographic grid cells. Larger circles represent more catalog entries in a cell—not more proven impact events. Coordinates are aggregated into 2° grid cells to reduce overplotting.

No records match these filters: this historical dataset contains no mappable Antarctic-latitude records classified as witnessed falls. Select “Found later” or “Found and Fell” to view the recovery map.
This is a map of recorded geographic discoveries. It does not measure fall frequency, search effort, or area-adjusted impact probability.

04 What is missing from the map?

Some coordinates are absent or unsuitable. All 45,716 records remain in the analysis; only the geographic map excludes unusable positions.

13,530 entries have unusable coordinates: 7,315 missing, 6,214 at placeholder (0,0), and one invalid Earth coordinate.

The bigger lesson

A map of discoveries is not necessarily a map of where events occur. Antarctica's cold, dry conditions, ice concentration mechanisms, and targeted scientific searches help explain why recovered meteorites are so well represented in the catalog. The dataset by itself does not prove where meteorites fall most frequently.

Source: NASA Open Data Meteorite Landings, historical snapshot (primarily through 2013), originating from Meteoritical Society records. Antarctic latitudes defined here as ≤ −60°. Each observation is a catalog record, not necessarily an independent fall.
